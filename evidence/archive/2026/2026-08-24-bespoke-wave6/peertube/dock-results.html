the owner decision, and what it costs

Is a network-fetched item TITLE permitted resting text — a NAME, or third-party content the app did not author? Shared with archivist, applies to nothing else in the fleet.

This spec rules NAME, on the shipped, deny-by-default-rostered cameraInput entry, which classifies a runtime-enumerated device label as "a name, not a measurement". A camera's label is also content the app did not author, arriving from outside the process, different on every machine.

⚠ The stakes are higher here than on the sibling. archivist shows one title. peertube's entire interaction is choosing among twenty-four. Under the other reading the list becomes a column of 64 × 36 thumbnails with a hostname under each — not a smaller feature, a different and much worse one. Neither face is BLOCKED either way.

the progress bar becomes a control

The card's :613 is a read-only <div class="bar"> — a derived value painted at rest with nothing under it, which is the shape the resting-text ruling refuses. Its sibling has had a real <input type="range"> over the same quantity all along.

The honest fix and the compliant fix are the same fix, for the third time in this program. The position IS the value; the timecode lives on aria-valuetext.

⚠ HLS seeking is not free, and M4 must answer it before the body is written: a live stream has no meaningful duration and hls.js seeking is bounded by the buffered range. The fallback is honest — keep the bar read-only and the control disabled for isLive, exactly as the sibling disables its seek at durationSec <= 0.
