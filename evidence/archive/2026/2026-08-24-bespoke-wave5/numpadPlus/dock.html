Every string on that plate has a permitted role, and the hard case is argued rather than assumed. A key cap is ONE control: its caption is the note it plays (c#) and its option name is the physical key bound to it (1, /, Q). Neither is a quantity, neither restates a dial position, and both are the only thing separating fourteen identical caps. The bound key is an ENGRAVING — what is printed on the key in front of the player — which is the cvBuddy.ppqn argument ("there is no name for the state that is not the integer") reached by a panel instead of a knob. Delete it and remapping becomes write-only. spec.md §7 carries the full test and the boundary.
The octave NUMBER is gone; the octave is still readable. The card paints {octave} as a decimal (NumpadPlusCard.svelte:218). The face gives octave an options roster labelled c0…c8 — derived from the module's own arithmetic (midiForKey puts octave N's C at (N+1)*12, and noteNameForMidi names it), so it is what the state is CALLED rather than a reading of it. That also avoids nine NUMERIC_LABEL_EXEMPTIONS entries in a list whose own header says "POPULATE IT FROM A SWEEP, NOT FROM THE RED LINE".

why the grid is the HERO and not rank 7

panelTierProblems refuses a panel SELECTED at any lane tier, and the plate cap is PLATE_COLS × PLATE_MAX_ROWS = 6 — so a panel's first legal ordinary rank is 7. PF-22's laneOrder drops face.hero.cell from the LANE roster only, so a hero picture costs no lane rank and may rank first. kria is that fix's first adopter; this is its second.

Neither declares optionsExhaustive — both rosters cover every integer step, and param-vocabulary reddens a redundant clause. ⚠ The inverse of wave 4's midiclock finding, checked rather than copied.
