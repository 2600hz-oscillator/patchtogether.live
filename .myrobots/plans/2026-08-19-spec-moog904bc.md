## A.3 THE RANKING ARGUMENT, FROM THE DSP

Read `packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:132-160`. Per sample:

```
cutoffKnob = smCutoff.step(cutoffRaw)          // :143  80 Hz one-pole, on the RAW dial
cutoffHz   = cutoffKnob * rangeMul * 2^cutoffCv // :144
if (cutoffHz < 4) cutoffHz = 4                  // :145
else if (cutoffHz > 20000) cutoffHz = 20000     // :146
y = x; for (4 stages) y = hpDerive(y, ladder[s].step(y, cutoffHz, 0, 0), 1)  // :154-158
```

- **`cutoff` is rank 1.** It is the only continuous control, it is the only
  thing in the per-sample expression that a player moves musically, and every
  other quantity multiplies *it*.
- **`range` is rank 2, and the demotion is the defensible part.** It is
  `k-rate`, read ONCE per block (`:127-129`) — declared, in the source, as *"a
  switch, not a swept value"*. It MULTIPLIES what rank 1 already does. Its
  consequence belongs in a READOUT, not in a rank.
- **There is no rank 3.** `k = 0` and `drive = 0` are literals at `:156`, not
  params. The 904B has no resonance pot and cannot self-oscillate
  (`moog904b.test.ts:157` asserts silence-in ⇒ silence-out).

**THE TEST THAT WOULD BE WRONG FOR A DIFFERENT MODULE.** This ranking is
justified by `range` being *k-rate and multiplicative*. It is **wrong** for
`moog921b`'s `RANGE` (octave footage — that is the pitch, i.e. rank 1 for an
oscillator), and it is **wrong** for `moog904a`'s `regeneration`, which is
rank 2 there because it is a *change of kind* (filter → oscillator), not a
multiplier. Apply "a multiplier ranks below what it multiplies" only where the
multiplied thing is itself a control the player reaches for.

## A.4 THE FINDING — a dial with a dead band at BOTH ends, at DIFFERENT ends

Two clamps sit between the dial and the filter, and they are in **different
files**:

| clamp | value | file:line |
|---|---|---|
| worklet, on the PRODUCT | `[4, 20000]` Hz | `packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:145-146` |
| shared ladder, on what it receives | `[10, sampleRate·0.49]` Hz | `packages/dsp/src/lib/moog-ladder-dsp.ts:114-119` (`fmin = 10`) |

The **10 Hz** floor is the one nobody wrote down. It is lower than the worklet's
4 and lower than the def's `min: 4`, so it never appears in any doc, any test,
or the module manifest — and it makes the declared minimum unreachable.

**DERIVED-BY-READING.** Dial is log `4 … 20000`, so its total travel in log
space is `ln(20000/4) = ln 5000 = 8.5171932`.

| RANGE | multiplier | dead span | fraction of the dial | which end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 = LOW** | ×1 | dial `4 … 10` Hz all deliver **10 Hz** | `ln(10/4) / ln 5000` = **10.758 %** | **BOTTOM** |
| **2 = HIGH** | ×2<sup>1.5</sup> = 2.8284271 | dial `7071.068 … 20000` Hz all deliver **20 000 Hz** | `ln(2.8284271) / ln 5000` = `1.5·ln2 / ln 5000` = **12.207 %** | **TOP** |

Boundaries land exactly on `10 ÷ 1` and `20000 ÷ 2^1.5`. Cross-checks: at HIGH
the bottom is live (`4 × 2.8284 = 11.31 > 10`); at LOW the top is live
(`20000 × 1 = 20000`, exactly at the ceiling, zero span).

> **There is no RANGE position at which the whole CUTOFF dial is live, and the
> two positions kill opposite ends.** `moog904a` has neither property (its
> RANGE 1 is fully live, and all its dead travel is at the top).

⚠ **CAVEAT INHERITED FROM Q39, AND IT APPLIES HERE UNCHANGED.** `smCutoff`
(`moog904b.ts:143`) smooths the **RAW dial in Hz** *before* the multiply and the
clamp, so two dial positions that settle to one filter **travel there
differently**. Any bit-comparison must be on the **SETTLED TAIL**; a
whole-buffer comparison will report a smaller dead fraction and look like a
disproof. The Q39 write-up records exactly this trap
(`strict-faces.ts:1785-1791`).

## A.6 GLYPH — it BINDS, and the resolver names the tap

`moog904bDef.outputs` is `[{ id: 'audio', type: 'audio' }]` (`moog904b.ts:54`).
`primaryAudioOutPortId` is `def?.outputs?.find(o => o.type === 'audio')?.id ?? null`
(`shell-glyph-live.ts:95-97`), so it **returns `'audio'`** — not null. With
`glyph: 'waveform'` and no `shape1`/`pw`/`mix` triple, `glyphBinding` falls to

**And it is pixel-deterministic at spawn, on a stronger argument than 904a's.**

**`'waveform'` over `'meter'`:** both resolve to the identical binding, so the

## A.7 THE READOUT, AND ITS NEGATIVE CONTROLS

New file `packages/web/src/lib/ui/modules/moog904b-face-model.ts`, registered in
`face-readout-values.ts` as `'moog904b-cutoff-hz'`.

```
moog904bCutoffHz(p) = clamp(p.cutoff * rangeMul904b(p.range), 10, 20000)
rangeMul904b(r)     = Math.round(r) >= 2 ? 2 ** 1.5 : 1
```

Note the **lower bound is 10, not 4** — the ladder's `fmin`, not the worklet's.
Printing `4 Hz` would be printing a frequency the filter never runs at.

**EXACTNESS, stated precisely.** The formula is exact and rate-independent for
every sample rate **≥ 20000 / 0.49 = 40 816.33 Hz**, which covers 44 100 and
48 000. Below that the ladder's own `sampleRate · 0.49` ceiling
(`moog-ladder-dsp.ts:116`) governs instead of 20 000 and the readout would
over-read. `FaceReadoutValue` is `(read: (paramId) => number | undefined) => string`
(`face-readout-values.ts:404`) and cannot reach the sample rate, so this
window is the honest claim — state it in the model's doc comment.

### ⚠ NO −3 dB CORNER READOUT — rejected on arithmetic, not skipped

Four cascaded one-pole high-passes (`HP_STAGES = 4`, `moog904b.ts:78`, each
`hpDerive(…, 1)` = the exact TPT complement `x − lp1`) put the composite −3 dB
point at

```
r = 1 / sqrt(2^(1/4) − 1) = 2.298958   →  +1.20097 octaves ABOVE the delivered cutoff
```

**DERIVED-BY-READING.** So the dial is not the corner in the first place. Worse,
the naive form ignores the `tan` prewarp in `ladderCutoffToG`
(`moog-ladder-dsp.ts:118`). At 48 kHz:

| delivered fc | naive `2.298958 · fc` | prewarped `(sr/π)·atan(2.298958·tan(π·fc/sr))` | naive error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 000 Hz | 2 298.96 Hz | **2 285.0 Hz** | −0.61 % |
| 20 000 Hz | 45 979.2 Hz | **22 228.9 Hz** | **+106.8 %** |

All DERIVED-BY-READING. And the decisive one:

> **The naive corner exceeds Nyquist for the top 7.63 % of the dial at RANGE
> LOW and the top 19.84 % at RANGE HIGH.** (Naive corner > 24 000 Hz when
> delivered fc > 24000/2.298958 = 10 439.6 Hz; that is dial ≥ 10 439.6 at LOW —
> `ln(20000/10439.6)/ln 5000 = 7.634 %` — and dial ≥ 3 690.9 at HIGH —
> `ln(20000/3690.9)/ln 5000 = 19.842 %`.)

A readout that prints an above-Nyquist frequency over a fifth of the dial is not
a readout. **This rejection is SHARPER than 904a's**: 904a's corner sits *below*
the delivered cutoff (×0.435), moving *away* from Nyquist; 904b's sits *above*
it (×2.299), moving *into* Nyquist. So the error is worse at every setting and
the failure is qualitative rather than quantitative. Record the rejection in the
face model, the way `moog904a-face-model.ts:104-129` does.

### The readout's negative controls (BOTH directions, one permanent leg)

1. **PERMANENT NEGATIVE LEG — it is blind to the CV jack, by design.**
   `cutoff_cv` is a node input, not an AudioParam: the worklet applies
   `Math.pow(2, cutoffCv)` per sample at `moog904b.ts:144`, the def marks it
   PASSTHROUGH with no `cvScale` (`moog904b.ts:48-52`), and
   `art/scenarios/cv-terminal/cv-terminal.test.ts:182` names the exclusion in
   those words. A param reader cannot see it. **Assert it: patch a constant
   +1 V into `cutoff_cv` and the readout must NOT move.** That is what proves
   the readout is a param derivation and not a claim about the live filter — and
   the doc must say "unpatched delivered cutoff".
2. **POSITIVE control, RANGE:** dial 1000, RANGE LOW → `1.0 kHz`; RANGE HIGH →
   `2.8 kHz`. The multiplier must be visible in one toggle.
3. **POSITIVE control, the TOP pin:** at RANGE HIGH, dial 7071 and dial 20000
   must BOTH print `20.0 kHz`, and dial 6900 (2.4 % below the boundary) must
   print `19.5 kHz`. The 2 %-below leg is the instrument's own control.
4. **POSITIVE control, the BOTTOM pin — this one has NO analogue on 904a:** at
   RANGE LOW, dial 4 and dial 9 must BOTH print `10 Hz`, and dial 11 must print
   `11 Hz`.
5. **INVARIANCE:** nothing else exists to move it. There are two params and both
   feed this one number.

### Why there is NO second readout

904a shipped two because `regeneration` existed and crossed a change of *kind*.
904b has two params and **both feed the same expression**, so a second readout
would restate the first. The only other candidate — the 24 dB/oct slope — is a
constant and a number. **One readout. Say so explicitly in the PR**, so a
reviewer does not read the asymmetry with 904a as an omission.

## A.8 DEFECTS FOUND — `moog904b`

**B-1 · THE SHIPPING DOCS DESCRIBE A TOPOLOGY THE DSP EXPLICITLY REJECTED.
(High — it is user-facing prose in three places.)**

`packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:11-19` is an IMPLEMENTATION NOTE saying, in the
shipping source: *"a single subtraction `input − lp4` does NOT give a clean
24 dB/oct high-pass … leaves a resonant bump near cutoff + a shallow ~6 dB/oct
stopband (measured: only ~−8 dB an octave below cutoff). So we CASCADE FOUR
one-pole high-pass stages."* The code does that (`:78`, `:154-158`,
`hpDerive(y, taps, 1)`).

Three places still tell the user the rejected version:

- `packages/web/src/lib/audio/modules/moog904b.ts:5-6` (def header comment):
  *"built by subtracting the ladder's low-passed signal from the input
  (input − lp4 → the complementary 4-pole high-pass)"*
- `packages/web/src/lib/audio/modules/moog904b.ts:62` (`docs.explanation`,
  **user-visible**): *"(input − four-pole-lowpass = the complementary four-pole
  highpass)"*
- `packages/web/src/lib/docs/module-manifest.ts:226-227` (`DESCRIPTIONS`,
  **user-visible**): *"built (like the hardware) by SUBTRACTING the ladder's
  low-passed signal from the input (input - lp4 -> the complementary 4-pole
  high-pass)"*

Also `packages/dsp/src/lib/moog-ladder-dsp.ts:24-25` — the shared core's own
consumer list says 904B *"reuses the same ladder via hpDerive() (input minus the
low-passed signal → high-pass family)"*, which is true only for `pole = 1`, four
times, on four separate ladder instances.

This is the "a comment claims sameness the code does not honour" class the brief
asks for, and it is the same claim repeated by three independent registries. Fix
all four in the face PR (boy-scout: `docs` is co-located and free of the attest,
`DESCRIPTIONS` is a shared list — sweep for conflicts after merge).

**B-2 · THE DECLARED CUTOFF MINIMUM (4 Hz) IS UNREACHABLE. (High.)**
`min: 4` (`moog904b.ts:56`), `minValue: 4` (`packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:106`),
worklet floor 4 (`:145`) — but `ladderCutoffToG`'s `fmin = 10`
(`moog-ladder-dsp.ts:115`) clamps every one of the four stages. **The bottom
10.758 % of the CUTOFF dial is bit-exactly one filter at RANGE LOW**
(DERIVED-BY-READING, A.4). Nothing in the def, the docs, the DESCRIPTIONS entry
or any test mentions the 10 Hz floor. The readout in A.7 makes it visible; the
`controls.cutoff` doc should state it.

**B-3 · THE `range` DOC SAYS "THE SAME SWEEP SHIFTED UP", AND IT IS NOT. (Med.)**
`moog904b.ts:72`: *"HIGH (the same sweep shifted up about 1.5 octaves)"*. The
multiplier is applied BEFORE the ceiling (`packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:144-146`),
so at HIGH the sweep is shifted up **and truncated** — the top 12.207 % of the
travel is folded onto 20 kHz. Same falsehood shape as the one Q39 fixed on 904a's
`range` doc; 904a's replacement text is the model
(`moog904a.ts` `controls.range`).

**B-4 · AFTER PROMOTION, `range` RENDERS AS AN ANONYMOUS ROTARY. (High — a
promotion blocker, and it is BLIND to every current gate.)**
`paramCellKind` (`shell-control-kind.ts:264-271`) resolves: not momentary, not a
declared cell, **no `options`**, then `looksLikeToggle(p)` — which is
`p.curve === 'discrete' && p.min === 0 && p.max === 1`
(`group-controls.ts:54-56`). `range` is `min: 1, max: 2`, so it is **not** a
toggle and falls through to `'knob'`. `KnobConic` does round for `discrete`
(`knob-conic-model.ts:71-72`), so the committed value stays integral — but with
no roster `paintsReadout` is false, so **nothing is painted**: the user gets an
unlabelled dial whose left half is LOW and right half is HIGH, replacing a
captioned two-button radiogroup. The `options[]` in A.5 is the fix. Note both
gates that should catch this are blind — `module-face-lint`'s completeness sees
the key is claimed, and the def-reading gates see a legal `discrete` param.

**B-5 · THE MULTIPLIER CANNOT BE IMPORTED, SO THE FACE MODEL MUST RE-STATE IT.
(Med — a drift risk with a priced fix.)**
`moog904a-face-model.ts:16` imports the real `rangeMultiplier` from the shipping
lib, *"so there is one source and nothing to drift"*.
**904b cannot do that**: `rangeMultiplier904b` and `RANGE_HIGH_MULT` are
module-local in the worklet (`packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:70-74`), and that
file top-level-exports nothing **by design** (`:32-37`,
`dsp-worklet-no-top-level-export`). Two options, both costed:

- **(a) re-state `2 ** 1.5` in the face model** with a comment naming

## B.3 THE RANKING ARGUMENT, FROM THE DSP

`packages/dsp/src/moog904c.ts:152-179`, per sample:

```
lpCutoff = clampCorner(cutoff * (1 + width))        // :161
hpCutoff = clampCorner(cutoff * (1 - width * 0.9))  // :162
lp = lpLadder.step(x, lpCutoff, 0, 0).lp4           // :166   24 dB/oct LP
bp = lp; for (4 stages) bp = hpDerive(bp, hpLadders[s].step(bp, hpCutoff, 0, 0), 1)  // :171-175
out = bp*(1 - mode) + (x - bp)*mode                 // :178
```

- **`cutoff` is rank 1.** It is the only `a-rate` param
  (`:117` vs `:120`/`:122` both `k-rate`), it is the only CV destination, and it
  MULTIPLIES both corners.
- **`width` is rank 2.** It is `k-rate`, but it is not a mere multiplier: it is
  the only control that changes the band's *shape*, and — see B.5 — it is what
  determines whether the module does anything audible at all.
- **`mode` is rank 3.** It is the last operation, a pure output crossfade
  applied after both filters have run. It cannot change *where* the band is, and
  its effect size is entirely set by rank 2.

**THE TEST THAT WOULD BE WRONG FOR A DIFFERENT MODULE.** "The output crossfade
ranks last" holds because `mode` here is genuinely terminal and post-filter. It
is **wrong** for `qbrt`'s `mode` and `resofilter`'s `mode`, which SELECT the
filter topology — those change what the earlier stages compute and rank at or
near the top. Rank a mode control last only when the source shows it applied
after everything it could have influenced.

## B.4 THE FINDING — the dial labelled "band CENTRE" is not the centre

The two corner laws are **asymmetric**, and the asymmetry is one literal:
`(1 + width)` up versus `(1 − width · 0.9)` down
(`packages/dsp/src/moog904c.ts:161-162`). The HP corner is pulled down 9× harder
than the LP corner is pushed up. The geometric centre of the band is therefore

```
centre = cutoff · sqrt((1 + width) · (1 − 0.9·width))
```

**DERIVED-BY-READING** (the peak of `|LP4|·|HP4|` sits at the geometric mean of
the two corners; at that frequency the two phase terms cancel to ≈0, so `H` is
real there — checked at `width` 0.5 and 1):

| WIDTH | centre ÷ cutoff | offset from the dial |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 1.000000 | 0 |
| 0.25 | 0.984251 | −0.0229 oct |
| **0.50 (SHIPPED DEFAULT)** | **0.908295** | **−0.1387 oct (−9.2 %)** |
| 0.75 | 0.754155 | −0.4069 oct |
| 1.00 | 0.447214 | **−1.1610 oct (−55.3 %)** |

> **`cutoff` is documented as "the band CENTRE" — `moog904c.ts:31` (def header),
> `:77` (`docs.controls.cutoff`, user-visible: *"the centre of the pass band"*),
> `:71` (`docs.inputs.cutoff_cv`: *"CV → the band CENTRE"*) — and it is the
> centre at exactly one setting, WIDTH = 0. At the shipped default it is already
> an eighth of an octave out; at full WIDTH it is out by more than an octave.**

This is 904b's finding in a different costume — a dial whose printed number is
not the quantity it names — but the mechanism is completely different (an
asymmetric corner law, not a range multiplier and a clamp), and **there is no
dead dial travel here at all**: see B.6.

## B.5 THE SECOND FINDING — both character controls are weakest where the docs call them strongest

With `k = 0` and `drive = 0` on both ladder chains (`:166`, `:173`), the whole
response is linear and closed-form. Let `ρ = lp/hp = (1 + w)/(1 − 0.9w)`. Then
the band-pass peak gain is exactly

```
|H|peak = (ρ / (1 + ρ))^4
```

**DERIVED-BY-READING.** And since `H` is real and positive at that frequency,
the MODE = 1 notch depth is exactly `1 − |H|peak`.

| WIDTH | ρ | BP peak (MODE 0) | BR notch depth (MODE 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 1.000 | **−24.08 dB** | **−0.56 dB** |
| 0.25 | 1.613 | −16.76 dB | −1.36 dB |
| **0.50 (DEFAULT)** | 2.727 | **−10.85 dB** | **−2.94 dB** |
| 0.75 | 5.385 | −5.92 dB | −6.12 dB |
| 1.00 | 20.000 | −1.70 dB | **−15.03 dB** |

(The two curves cross at `w = 0.744389`, where both are −3.01 dB.)

**MEASURED (committed artifact) — the derivation checks out.**
`fingerprints.generated.json` → `moog904c/audio.labels.peakDb = −17.39` dBFS,
against the same `vcoTestSignal` driver at `amp = 0.5` = **−6.0206 dBFS**
(`art/scenarios/moog904c/profile.test.ts:82`, `art/setup/drivers.ts:113`). That
is **−11.37 dB through the module** at the loudest point of a sweep whose stated
design is to cross the saw's fundamental (`profile.test.ts:11-16`) — i.e. at or
very near the band's peak, at the default WIDTH 0.5. **Derived says −10.85 dB.
They agree to 0.5 dB**, the residual being the saw's harmonic content and the
sweep not parking exactly on the optimum.

Three consequences, each a defect below:

1. `docs.controls.width` (`moog904c.ts:78`) sells low WIDTH as *"a narrow,
   **resonant**, vocal band"*. **There is no resonance anywhere in this module**
   — both `step()` calls pass `k = 0, drive = 0`. Narrowing does not sharpen a
   peak; it removes 24 dB. (C-1.)
2. `docs.controls.mode` (`:79`) says at MODE 1 *"the band is subtracted from the
   input so it is scooped OUT"*. At the shipped WIDTH that scoop is **2.94 dB**,
   and at WIDTH 0 it is **0.56 dB** — a near-perfect passthrough. **The deepest
   notch happens at the WIDEST band**, which is the opposite of what "notch"
   suggests. (C-2.)
3. The shipped default patch (`width 0.5, mode 0`) is a **−10.85 dB** insertion
   at its own peak. Not a defect on its own, but it is the number a face should
   let the user see.

⚠ **THE EXISTING TEST IS STRUCTURALLY BLIND TO ALL OF THIS.**
`moog904c.test.ts:350-358` asserts `centre < skirt` and `centre < 0.9` — a
RELATIVE comparison plus a 0.9 ceiling against a true value of **0.713**. It
would pass identically for a −40 dB notch and for a −0.5 dB one. It cannot
distinguish "band-reject" from "very slightly quieter in the middle". If the
face prints a depth, add a leg that pins the depth.

## B.9 THE READOUTS, AND THEIR NEGATIVE CONTROLS

New file `packages/web/src/lib/ui/modules/moog904c-face-model.ts`, two ids in
`face-readout-values.ts`.

```
moog904cLowHz(p)   = max(10, p.cutoff * (1 - 0.9 * p.width))            // Hz
moog904cSpanOct(p) = log2((1 + p.width) / (1 - 0.9 * p.width))          // octaves
```

Together they fully determine the band (`high = low · 2^span`), so the plate
carries the whole answer in two lines.

**EXACTNESS.** Both are exact and rate-independent for every sample rate
**≥ 40 816.33 Hz** (44.1 k and 48 k included): `low` is clamped only by the
ladder's rate-INDEPENDENT `fmin = 10` (`moog-ladder-dsp.ts:115`), and `span`
is invariant to `cutoff` entirely. Above that window they are exact; below it
the ladder's `sampleRate·0.49` ceiling can truncate the top edge and `span`
would over-read. State the window in the model.

### ⚠ A `centre` READOUT WAS DRAFTED AND REJECTED, on the same rule that killed 904a's corner

`centre = cutoff · sqrt((1+w)(1−0.9w))` is the module's headline (B.4) and it is
the obvious readout. It is rejected because it is the one derivation here that
**depends on the sample rate**: when `cutoff·(1+w) > sampleRate·0.49` the true
centre is `sqrt(clamp(lp)·hp)`, not `sqrt(lp·hp)`. At 48 kHz that window opens
at `cutoff > 15 680` at the default width (**3.52 %** of the log dial,
`ln(20000/15680)/ln 1000`), and at the extreme — `cutoff 20000, width 1` — the
printed value would be `sqrt(40000·2000) = 8944.3 Hz` against a true
`sqrt(23520·2000) = 6858.6 Hz`, an over-read of **+0.383 oct / +30.4 %**. All
DERIVED-BY-READING. `FaceReadoutValue` cannot reach the sample rate
(`face-readout-values.ts:404`), so a `centre` readout would have to hard-code
48 kHz and be silently wrong at 44.1 kHz — the exact reason Q39 refused the
corner formula. **Record the rejection in the face model** and put the centre
fact in the `band` page hint and in `docs.controls.cutoff` instead.

### Negative controls (disjoint reach is the mechanism)

|  | moves on `cutoff` | moves on `width` | moves on `mode` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `low` | **YES** | **YES** | never |
| `span` | **NEVER — this is its control** | **YES** | never |

1. **PERMANENT NEGATIVE LEG for `span`:** sweep `cutoff` across its whole
   declared range at fixed `width` and `span` must be **bit-identical** at every
   point. If `span` ever moves on `cutoff`, the clamp window has been entered and
   the readout is outside its stated exactness window — which is the only way it
   can be wrong, so this leg is also the window's alarm.
2. **PERMANENT NEGATIVE LEG for both:** move `mode` end to end. **Neither
   readout may move.** `mode` is a post-filter crossfade and touches neither
   corner (`moog904c.ts:178`); a readout that twitched would mean the model had
   copied the wrong expression.
3. **POSITIVE control, the asymmetry:** at `cutoff 800`, `width 0 → 1` must take
   `low` from `800 Hz` to `80 Hz` (÷10) while `span` goes `0.00 oct` →
   `4.32 oct` (`log2 20`). One gesture, both readouts, and the 9× asymmetry is
   visible in the pair.
4. **POSITIVE control, the floor:** `cutoff 20, width 1` → `low` must print
   `10 Hz`, not `2 Hz`. That pins the `fmin` clamp the def never mentions.
5. **BOTH readouts are blind to `cutoff_cv`**, and unlike 904b's jack this one is
   NOT passthrough — it is scaled and summed into the AudioParam. See C-3. State
   "unpatched" in the doc.

±4.98 oct. (High — and it is the single biggest behavioural difference between
904c and its two siblings.)**
`packages/web/src/lib/docs/module-manifest.ts:182-183` (user-visible
DESCRIPTIONS): *"plus a 1V/oct cutoff CV input"*. The def declares
`cvScale: { mode: 'log' }` (`moog904c.ts:57`) and routes the port to the `cutoff`
AudioParam (`:113`). `scaleCv`'s log branch is
`knob · (paramMax/paramMin)^(cv·depth/2)` (`cv-scale.ts:82-84`), so with
`min 20 / max 20000` a **±1 CV is a ×31.62 / ÷31.62 multiplier — ±4.98
octaves**, centred on the live knob. DERIVED-BY-READING, and independently
corroborated in the tree: `strict-faces.ts:550` already states *"38 of the
registry's 44 log-curve CV targets declare `log` (symmetric at ±4.98 oct),
including the qbrt and **moog904c** cutoffs."*

The def's own `docs.inputs.cutoff_cv` (`:71`) is correct (*"a bipolar ±1 CV
sweeps the band across its full octave span"*). **Only the DESCRIPTIONS entry is
wrong, and it is wrong in the one word a patcher acts on.** Compare
`moog904b`, whose jack genuinely IS 1 V/oct
(`Math.pow(2, cutoffCv)`, `packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:144`). Patch one LFO
into both and they behave completely differently; nothing on either module says
so.

**C-4 · `cutoff` IS UNSMOOTHED ON 904c AND SMOOTHED ON 904b. (Med — audible,
undocumented, and it is a face-relevant asymmetry because a face knob drags.)**
`packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:91-99, 143` runs the cutoff knob through an 80 Hz
`WtParamSmoother` precisely so *"CV zipper [stays] out of the audio path on knob
drags"*. `packages/dsp/src/moog904c.ts` has **no smoother at all** — `:157` reads
the a-rate `cutoff` array directly, and the factory writes it with
`setValueAtTime` (`moog904c.ts:105`, `:117`), i.e. a step. Two modules built in
the same batch, on the same core, one of them protected against the exact thing
the other is exposed to. Neither header mentions the difference. Flag it; do not
fix it in the face PR (it is a DSP change and an ART re-pin).

**C-5 · THE WORKLET HEADER'S OWN TOPOLOGY BLOCK IS WRONG. (Low.)**
`packages/dsp/src/moog904c.ts:17` writes the HP side as a single
`hpDerive(lp, MoogLadder(HP).step(lp, hpCutoff))`. The code cascades **four**
one-pole stages on four separate ladder instances (`:82`, `:171-175`), which the
same file describes correctly 60 lines later (`:79-82`). The pseudo-code block a
reader hits first is the wrong one. Same "a comment claims a topology the code
does not honour" class as B-1, contained to one file.

**C-6 · A DEAD `input: 0` IN THE FACTORY'S PORT MAP. (Low.)**
`moog904c.ts:113` declares both `input: 0` and `param` for `cutoff_cv`.
`engine.ts:432` takes the `param` branch and never reads `input`. Harmless, but
it reads as though the CV also lands in the audio input — delete it or comment
it.

**C-7 · `moog904b` AND `moog904c` ARE BOTH ABSENT FROM `EXPECTED_NODE_TYPES`.
(Low — a one-directional gate, not a red test.)**
`packages/web/src/lib/ui/modules-card-map.test.ts:37-63` is a hand-maintained
list whose own comment says *"When you add a NEW module, add its type id here
too"* and *"any NEW registered module must ship a card + be added to
EXPECTED_NODE_TYPES"*. Both modules are registered, both have cards, neither is
in the list (`moog904a` is, at `:50`). The test only asserts
`EXPECTED ⊆ resolved` and `registered ⊆ resolved` (`:102-105`, `:112-130`), never
`registered ⊆ EXPECTED`, so **the rule the file states cannot fail on its own
violation** — a gate whose stated scope exceeds what it can see. Not this PR's
job to fix the gate, but adding the two lines is free and honest.

## B.11 RISK, and what a builder must NOT assume — `moog904c`

- **Do NOT go looking for a RANGE switch or dead dial travel.** Neither exists
  (B.6). Finding nothing is the correct outcome, not a missed search.
- **Do NOT ship a `centre` readout** without re-reading B.9 — it is the
  attractive wrong answer here, exactly as the −3 dB corner was on 904a.
- **Do NOT declare `options` on `mode` or `width`.** Both are continuous
  crossfades; `options` would hide real blends and `param-vocabulary.test.ts`
  requires `discrete` anyway (`types.ts:388-391`).
- **Do NOT give `width` two endpoint landmarks** (the nearest-landmark trap,
  `knob-vocabulary-model.ts:103-110`).
- **Do NOT treat this module's CV jack like 904a's or 904b's.** It is a scaled
  AudioParam sum at ±4.98 oct, not a per-sample 1 V/oct multiply (C-3).
- **Do NOT weaken `moog904c.test.ts:350-358` further.** If the face prints a
  depth, that test needs a leg that can actually fail on depth (B.5).
- **Verify the contract-lock diff is EMPTY** after adding `landmarks` and
  `face` — same reasoning as A.9.
- **`params[].landmarks` is CODE for attest purposes** (only `docs`,
  `controlFamilies` and `face` are stripped by
  `scripts/attest-code-basis.ts`). Check the basis before assuming free.
- **VRT:** one new dock baseline. `moog904c` is in the strict MODULES set
  (`e2e/vrt/vrt-exemptions.ts:1203`). Scoped dispatch; count the committed files.
- **ART:** nothing here re-pins. Both scenarios hash DSP sources only
  (`dspSourceSha('moog904c.ts', 'lib/moog-ladder-dsp.ts')`,
  `art/scenarios/moog904c/profile.test.ts:143`), and neither `face`, `docs`,
  `options` nor `landmarks` on a **web** def is in that basis. **The one thing
  that WOULD re-pin all three 904 scenarios is moving `rangeMultiplier904b` into
  the shared lib** — see B-5, and do not do it in a face PR.

## C. WHAT THESE TWO SPECS OWE THE QUEUE (additions to §5 / §10 / §15)

1. **"Same core" is not "same story."** Three modules share `MoogLadder` and
   produce three unrelated findings: a range multiplier that eats the top of a
   dial (904a), a **floor in a shared helper that eats the bottom of a different
   dial and is not mentioned in any def, doc or test** (904b), and an asymmetric
   corner law that moves the band centre an octave away from the dial that names
   it (904c). **Re-derive the arithmetic per module; the shared file is where
   the divergence hides, not where it is resolved.**
2. **When a face model cannot IMPORT the law, say so at the top of the file.**
   904a's model opens by boasting that its multiplier is imported. 904b's must
   open by admitting its multiplier cannot be, and naming the line it copies
   (`packages/dsp/src/moog904b.ts:70`) and the price of fixing it properly
   (three ART re-pins). A re-stated constant with a named source and a stated
   price is a decision; a re-stated constant without one is drift.
3. **A "clean-room / built by X" sentence in a DSP header is a claim about a
   REJECTED implementation as often as an accepted one.** `moog904b`'s worklet
   documents, in the shipping source, that `input − lp4` does not work — and
   three other files still tell the user that is what the module does. **Grep
   the def, its `docs`, and `DESCRIPTIONS` for the topology sentence whenever a
   worklet header contains the words "does NOT".**
4. **A relative assertion cannot see an absolute failure.**
   `moog904c.test.ts:350` proves the notch centre is lower than its skirts and
   passes at 0.713 against a ceiling of 0.9. A −0.56 dB "notch" would pass it
   too. When a module's whole claim is *how much*, at least one leg must assert
   *how much*.
5. **A committed ART fingerprint is a free second instrument.** `peakDb` read
   against the driver's known amplitude corroborated a derived passband figure
   to 0.5 dB and independently exposed 904b's +6.16 dB overshoot — no render, no
   GPU, no CI. `packages/web/src/lib/art/fingerprints.generated.json` +
   `art/setup/drivers.ts` should be the first place a spec looks for a
   cross-check on a derived level.
